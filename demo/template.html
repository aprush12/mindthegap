What it catches

Six handoff failures that turn into readmissions

Each one is small on the day and expensive a week later. The screen exists to surface them while the hospital can still fix them.

1

Dose changed on the last day

The discharge list says torsemide 40 mg. The cardiology note from the day before says 60 mg. The SNF gives the old dose.

See Harold Jennings: flagged at all three facilities
2

Day-one drugs the pharmacy can't supply

Entresto, SGLT2 inhibitors and DOACs are often non-formulary or need prior authorization. Arrive after the pharmacy cutoff and doses get missed.

See Elena Mercado at Bayview: 7 first doses at risk
3

No daily-weights order

A heart failure patient arrives with weights mentioned in a note but never ordered, and no plan for what weight gain should trigger a call.

See Elena Mercado: two packet gaps
4

Equipment not there on arrival

A bariatric scale, home oxygen or CPAP that nobody arranged. Checked against what the facility has on hand.

Rule THR-03
5

Pending labs nobody follows up

A repeat potassium is pending at transfer with no named person to receive the result.

See Harold Jennings: FUP-01
6

No cardiology follow-up booked

Appointments must have a date, a location and transport, and the patient needs a PCP of record.

Rules FUP-02, FUP-03
How it works

The model reads. The rules decide.

Reading a long chart is where a language model helps. Deciding whether a facility can admit someone is not, so that part is plain code you can audit line by line.

Step 1 · Model

Answer 43 questions about the record

Claude reads the packet once and answers a fixed set of facility-agnostic questions: Is PASRR complete? What is the isolation status? Which doses changed in the last 72 hours? Each answer carries the page and the exact quote.

Quotes are matched back to the PDF text. A quote that isn't in the document is marked unverified.

Step 2 · Rules engine

Apply each facility's policy and capacity

A pure TypeScript engine scores those answers against each facility's 43 rules, formulary, pharmacy cutoff, emergency kit and tonight's bed and staffing snapshot.

Result per facility: accept, conditional_accept, blocked or decline, with resolve-by times.

Step 3 · Prioritize

Rank what matters tonight

Items are ordered by urgency: first doses at risk, blocks that must clear before travel, things the facility can't do, then items on a clock.

Each one comes with the exact question to put to the discharging hospital. Together they form the call sheet.

The model never decides admissibility. If the model's summary cites something the engine didn't produce, it is dropped. If the model is unavailable, the same summary is built from the engine output alone.

Who it helps

One screen for both sides of the handoff

SNF admissions coordinator

  • Decide on a referral in minutes instead of reading 60 pages at 7 PM.
  • See why a patient can't come here, so the answer to the hospital is specific.
  • Every "no" or "not yet" has a page reference to back it up.

Hospital case manager

  • Learn which facility can take the patient tonight, and which can't at any level of paperwork.
  • Get one list of missing documents across all facilities instead of three phone calls.
  • Fix gaps before the ambulance is booked, not after a refused admission.

SNF nursing and pharmacy

  • Know which first doses are at risk and when they are due.
  • Get dose conflicts in front of a prescriber before the first administration.
  • Arrive with monitoring orders, equipment and follow-up already confirmed.
Built for clinical review

Designed to be checked, not trusted blindly

Unknown is not met

A trach that is never mentioned and a trach the reader missed look identical. When the packet doesn't answer a question, the screen says so and applies the facility's rule for missing information.

Contradictions are shown, not resolved

When two passages disagree, both quotes and both pages are surfaced side by side. A clinician picks the right one.

Fixed-length audit trail

Every facility gets the same 43 rows, including the ones that passed. Nothing is summarized away.

Policy lives in data

Rules, formularies and thresholds are JSON files per facility. Capacity is a separate snapshot updated each shift.

What's next

An agent that works the call sheet

Every open item already carries the one-sentence question for the hospital. The next step is a voice agent that calls the discharging unit, asks each question, and records the answers back against the rule that raised it.
